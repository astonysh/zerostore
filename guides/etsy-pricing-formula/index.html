The Etsy Pricing Formula for Handmade Sellers

Most handmade sellers price by multiplying materials, then wonder where the money went. Labour and Etsy's fees are the two things a materials multiple hides. Here is the formula that puts both back, with a calculator and a table that shows what each markup really earns.

Fee rates checked against Etsy's published policy, October 2026.

The formula:

Price = (materials + packaging + labour + shipping you pay + fixed fees) ÷ (1 − % fees − target margin) − shipping charged

% fees are Etsy's 6.5% transaction fee plus payment processing (US: 3%), so about 9.5% before ads, currency conversion or country surcharges. Fixed fees are the $0.20 listing fee plus the $0.25 card fee — about $0.45 per order that no percentage can cover.

Why "2× materials" loses money

A low multiple is not automatically wrong — it is wrong if you did not know the margin it produces. Note that the multiple needed rises as your hourly rate rises, which is why online "3× materials" rules of thumb disagree with each other.

The five numbers people forget

  1. Labour. If you do not price your own hours, you are donating them. Pick a rate you would pay someone else and keep it in the formula.
  2. Packaging. Box, tape, tissue, sticker, insert card. Small per item, real across a year.
  3. The fixed part of fees. $0.20 + $0.25 per order is 4.5% of a $10 item and 0.9% of a $50 one. Cheap items lose to fixed costs — this is why bundles and multi-item listings beat a single cheap SKU.
  4. Refunds and remakes. A single remake can wipe the margin on several orders. If 3% of your orders get remade, your true margin is lower than the table says.
  5. Ad-attributed orders. An Offsite Ad sale adds 15% or 12% on top of everything above. If a meaningful share of your orders come from ads, either price with it in mind or opt out while you are eligible.

Worked example: a $27 candle

Materials and packaging $6.50, 24 minutes of work at $18/hour ($7.20), postage $6.00 charged to the buyer at cost, 30% target margin, no ads:

(6.50 + 7.20 + 6.00 + 0.45) ÷ (1 − 0.095 − 0.30) = 33.31
33.31 − 6.00 shipping = $27.31 for the item

Check it: the buyer pays $33.31. Etsy takes $0.45 + 9.5% = $3.61. Your costs are $19.70. Profit $10.00, which is 30.0% of revenue. That is the number the formula was solving for — if you price on instinct and land on $19.99, the same order nets about $1.10.

These are arithmetic, not a forecast. Your margins depend on your costs, your market and how often you actually sell — a price that hits 30% on paper still makes $0 if nobody buys.

Questions sellers actually ask

What is the pricing formula for handmade items on Etsy?

Price = (materials + packaging + labour + shipping you pay + fixed fees) ÷ (1 − percentage fees − target margin) − shipping you charge. Percentage fees are about 9.5% in the US before ads; fixed fees are about $0.45 per order.

What profit margin should I aim for?

25–40% net after all fees and your own costs is a common working range. Below 20% a single refund or remake eats the order.

Should shipping be in the price or charged separately?

Etsy's percentage fees are the same either way — see the shipping calculator. Choose based on what converts in your market.

How do I price for a sale or wholesale?

Solve the formula with the target margin at 0% to get break-even, then check that your sale price still clears it. Etsy sale events stack on top of every other fee.

Does this work for digital products?

Yes — set materials, packaging and postage to $0. The fixed fees then dominate, which is why digital products want higher prices or bundles. For that case the platform fee calculator is a better fit.
